# Pre-run review of the method candidates (Fable 5.2, 2026-11-10)

Scope: the `fiducial`, `m3`, `fiducial_hybrid` and `envelope` arms of
[`simulation_spec.md`](simulation_spec.md), read against the current code, the
research record, and the theory doc. Theory additions are in
[`fiducial_band_theory.md`](fiducial_band_theory.md) §6.6, §12.1a, and short
notes in §6.2, §11.2, §13. No code was changed.

## 1. Bottom line

Nothing found invalidates the run. Launch it, after deciding on the four small
items in §2.

The main new result is an explanation of why the fiducial band over-covers
(.72 at nominal .50, .98 at nominal .95 on benign shapes). The band is centred
on a kernel smooth of the empirical ROC with bandwidth about $n_0^{-1/2}$, so
the truth sits more smoothly inside the cloud than the draws that set the trim
depth. Computing the depth from smoothed draws puts interior coverage on
nominal at all three α, with 8–17% less width at 500/500. That is a candidate
arm, not a pre-run change: the same surplus is what currently covers part of
the hybrid's unbounded exterior risk.

For M3, rounding the two harmful corners of its class rectangle is exact and
gives 4–8% less area on balanced smooth shapes, but 2–8% more on heavy tails.
A ceiling argument shows no region of this kind gets within 20% of the
fiducial tube's width. M3 as specified is the right exact arm.

Because every combination regenerates from its seed, a new rank-space arm can
be added later as a paired rerun without the bootstrap. Neither candidate
needs to hold up the run.

### 2a. The α = .05 draw budget, measured

Binormal AUC .8, one dataset per size, 12 seeds, one thread:

| $n_0=n_1$ | Draws | Depth | Seed SD of mean width | Mean width | Time | Cloud |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| 150 | 3,731 | 4–5 | .95% | .2363 | .04 s | 2 MB |
| 150 | 20,000 | 21–23 | .58% | .2348 | .21 s | 12 MB |
| 500 | 5,158 | 3–4 | .96% | .1151 | .18 s | 10 MB |
| 500 | 20,000 | 13–15 | .45% | .1146 | .73 s | 40 MB |
| 1,500 | 6,936 | 3–4 | 1.64% | .0743 | .78 s | 42 MB |
| 1,500 | 20,000 | 9–11 | .66% | .0740 | 2.34 s | 120 MB |
| 5,000 | 9,599 | 4 | .86% | .0409 | 2.86 s | 192 MB |
| 5,000 | 20,000 | 8–9 | .60% | .0406 | 6.56 s | 400 MB |

The current budget is not a coverage risk: the audit found .950 against .948
and .998 against .998 at 50/50 when going to 20,000 draws. What it costs is
reproducibility of a single band (about 1% in mean width, 3–4.5% at a point),
a depth that moves in steps of 25–33% of the local level, and a margin of one
or two above the $j<3$ fallback. An earlier draft of this report said the
local level "varies about ±50% between seeds"; that figure is the pointwise
tail probability cut by an extreme order statistic, not the band's realized
level, which varied 0–13%.

Things to know rather than change:

- **At n = 10 and 30 the hybrid is M3.** The floor region is the whole grid at
  5/5 and 88–96% of it at 15/15; it is still 41–49% at 50/50. Those cells
  compare M3 with itself. At 15/15 the hybrid is 40% wider than the raw band
  (.762 vs .543 at α = .05), which covers .988 there.
- **The thinned trim grid at n = 10,000 is benign.** Thinning can only narrow
  the band (now a stated corollary of Prop. 10b). On 300 paired replicates at
  5,000/5,000 it cost .6–1.7% width and at most 1.3 points of coverage.
- **Recalibration with a tiny exterior.** When only one or two columns lie
  outside the floor region, the recalibrated depth makes the band there a
  pointwise $(1-\alpha)$ interval. Coverage stayed above nominal in all 27
  hybrid cells the audit ran, so this is a structural note. A minimum exterior
  size before recalibrating would remove it.
- **`1-(1-0.2)**1.0` is 0.19999999999999996**, so the depth index at α = .2 is
  399, not 400. One draw. Pass α directly when C = 1.
- **Grid evaluation is not exact for M3.** Its steps sit at Beta quantiles, not
  at $k/n_0$. Grid versus all-$t$ coverage at α = .5 was .993/.985 (15/15),
  .996/.988 (50/50), .990/.988 (500/500). One sentence in the paper.
- **The envelope runner recomputes shared work per α.** `envelope_band_suite`
  already shares it; the runner calls `envelope_bootstrap_band` three times.
  Runtime only.

## 3. Fiducial band

### 3.1 Why it over-covers (theory doc §6.6)

A draw at FPR $t$ reads the rank path at a randomly shifted FPR,
$t+\beta_b(t)$ with $\beta_b\approx N(0,t(1-t)/n_0)$. Averaging over draws
makes the cloud centre a kernel smooth of the empirical ROC at that bandwidth
(11 grid cells at $t=.5$, $n_0=500$). So:

- truth minus centre is a *smoothed* sampling-error path;
- draw minus centre is a full-roughness path.

The rank trim is calibrated on the rough paths. A smooth path's supremum lacks
the fine-scale excursions, so the truth exits less often than a draw. The
cutoff shift is of order $n^{-1/4}$ in normal-score units, which is why the
surplus fades so slowly; there is a smaller $n^{-1/2}$ variance term as well.

Measured:

- The truth's standardized deviation is 33–50× smoother than a draw's at lag
  one grid cell, 12–15× at lag 4, 3–4× at lag 16.
- The raw tube trimmed and checked on FPR (.1, .9) misses at about .02, .10,
  .28 for α = .05, .2, .5 (500/500, diagonal and binormal .8). The truth's
  worst excursion is distributed over FPR like the draws' own. The surplus is
  uniform along the curve, not an end effect, which matches the record's
  failed end-geometry explanation.
- At 5,000/5,000 the production band covers .667 / .883 / .963.

This answers the research record's open item on interior conservatism (§7.1,
§9.10) and explains why fixed $C>1$ looked usable at moderate $n$ and fails
asymptotically.

### 3.2 A depth rule that removes the surplus (candidate arm)

Smooth each standardized draw with the same kernel, compute those
pseudo-truths' depths against the unsmoothed cloud, and take the usual
quantile. The tube is unchanged in form; only the depth is larger.

| Shape, sizes | α | Interior miss, production depth | Interior miss, smooth-truth depth | Width ratio |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| Binormal .8, 500/500 | .5 / .2 / .05 | .304 / .104 / .032 | .524 / .208 / .060 | .83 / .87 / .91 |
| Heteroskedastic (σ ratio 3), 500/500 | .5 / .2 / .05 | .300 / .116 / .040 | .440 / .172 / .056 | .85 / .89 / .92 |
| Binormal .95, 150/150 | .5 / .2 / .05 | .320 / .112 / .020 | .544 / .208 / .048 | .76 / .81 / .86 |

250 replicates per cell; "interior" is at least 8 ranks from each end on both
axes; standard errors about .03 at α = .5 and .015 at .05.

Limits of this evidence:

- Three benign shapes, two sizes, no imbalance, nothing at $n_0\ge$ 1,500.
- Interior only. Applied to all columns without standardization the kernel is
  wrong at the ends and gave a smaller depth at α = .05.
- It removes slack the hybrid currently leans on. The default hybrid's lowest
  measured cells are .948–.960 at α = .05. A calibrated interior plus an
  unbounded exterior will sit below nominal in those cells unless the region
  or the floor absorbs it.

Recommended next step: a paired arm on the complement of the floor region,
same cloud as the default hybrid, on the closure/split cell library including
the red team. If it holds, it is the direct answer to "calibrated across α".

### 3.3 Smaller points

- The Clopper–Pearson upper allowance is not what makes the band wide. It
  binds at 16–69% of columns but changes mean width by under 1% at 50/50 and
  500/500, and by up to 2.8% at 15/15 (5 replicates per cell, a width check
  only).
- Integer depths make the realized trimmed fraction .044–.045 at α = .05.
  Interpolating between depths $j$ and $j+1$ would recover 1–3% width. With
  item 2 of §2 the effect mostly disappears on its own.

## 4. M3

### 4.1 What the audit confirmed

Calibration is exact (dynamic program within 2e-5 of target; brute-force Monte
Carlo agrees at n = 15, 50, 2,000). Both index maps in the composition were
re-derived by hand. Shape checks passed on perfect and reverse separation, all
ties, 1/9, 9/1, 900/100 with $t(1.1)$. Calibration at 5,000/5,000 takes 5–12 s
per α once; band calls are then free.

### 4.2 Rounded class regions and their ceiling (theory doc §12.1a)

M3's upper edge reads only lower bounds on negative anchors and upper bounds
on positive anchors; the lower edge reads the other two. Of the four corners
of each class pair's rectangle, two cost width and two do not. Any lower set
$\mathcal D$ for the pair of one-sided maxima gives an exact band (Prop. 12h),
computed as the hull of a few M3 compositions and calibrated exactly by the
existing dynamic program. M3 is the square.

Half-width where the two sampling errors are equal, in units of the direct
ROC standard deviation:

| $n_0=n_1$, α | Square (M3) | $\ell_4$ region | Quarter disc | Ceiling for any region | Fiducial tube |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| 50, .05 | 4.53 | 3.92 | 3.68 | 3.57 | 2.90 |
| 500, .05 | 4.86 | 4.25 | 4.05 | 3.96 | 3.16 |
| 500, .5 | 3.56 | 3.19 | 3.06 | 2.98 | 2.18 |

The ceiling is not zero-cost to approach: where one class dominates, the disc
is 15–22% wider than the square and the $\ell_4$ region 3–7% wider. Paired
band areas relative to M3 (300 replicates per cell, calibration by 400,000
simulated order-statistic vectors, so not yet exact):

| Shape | 50/50, α = .05 | 500/500, α = .05 | 500/500, α = .5 | 900/100, α = .05 |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| Diagonal | .956 | .915 | .941 | .955 |
| Binormal .8 | .962 | .923 | .950 | .961 |
| Binormal .95 | .998 | .946 | .972 | .984 |
| Heteroskedastic | .978 | .940 | .966 | .990 |
| Shifted $t_2$ | 1.075 | 1.020 | 1.036 | 1.030 |

($\ell_4$ region; the quarter disc reaches .893 on the diagonal and 1.16–1.21
on shifted $t_2$.)

Reading: this is the earlier Fisher-product screen (.93–1.12) with a better
exchange rate, and the same sign pattern. It is a defensible optional exact
arm for a paper that wants M3's best balanced-case width. It does not change
the ranking, and the floor should keep the square, because the floor acts
where one class dominates. The ceiling is the more useful output: the
remaining 20–27% between any composition and the fiducial tube is the cost of
not knowing which negative rank faces which positive rank, and only test
inversion can recover it.

The same section states M3 and the fiducial tube on one axis: both are hulls
over a probability-$(1-\alpha)$ set of auxiliary spacings; M3 fixes the set
before seeing the labels, the fiducial tube lets the labels choose it.

Observations:

- At 50/50 the hybrid is wider than the raw band and covers less at α = .5
  (.735 vs .785). That is the recalibrated depth spending the interior surplus
  of §3.1. It is consistent with the closure/split report; it is worth saying
  plainly in the paper, because "floor added, coverage fell" reads oddly
  without the mechanism.
- The left cut depends on $(j, M)$, so the region depends on the Monte Carlo
  budget (region fraction .45 → .43 and width .3092 → .3054 at 50/50, α = .2,
  when $M$ goes 2,000 → 20,000). A rule in the local level alone,
  $p_k\le c\,j/M$, would be budget-free.
- The hybrid's stated total bound is $\alpha_{M3}$ plus the exterior term, and
  after recalibration the exterior gets the full α again. The docstrings say
  so; the spec's §6 sentence about the in-region bound is accurate.

I have no change to recommend to the hybrid's defaults for this run.

## 6. Bootstrap envelope

The audit found no coverage-breaking bug. What the method is, by size:

- **Small n: a Beta-floor band.** The floor rewrites 89–97% of interior lower
  points at $n_0$ = 15 and 64–67% at $n_0$ = 50. That, not the bootstrap, gives
  .93–.94 coverage at nominal .50, with every miss above.
- **Large n: a B-dependent envelope plus a noise-gated floor.** Coverage falls
  with n (prior run, pooled: .794 / .926 / .973 at n = 10,000 for α = .5 / .2 /
  .05; Student-t .667 / .879 / .967), with interior misses outside any floor.
- **Without floors it is degenerate on the TPR plateau.** Every retained curve
  equals 1 there, so the lower edge is exactly 1 and misses 80–96% of the time.
  A percentile-type envelope cannot leave the bootstrap support.

Opportunities, most useful first:

1. **Beta floor divisor** (§2 item 3). A code/doc mismatch; fix it.
2. **B as a declared parameter** (§2 item 4).
3. **Add the classical sup-t band as an arm.** $\widehat R\pm c\,\hat\sigma$
   with the same Wilson-floored standard deviation and no other floors, from
   the same bootstrap matrix at no extra cost. On binormal .8 it was within
   .04 of nominal at α = .2 and .05 in all nine cells and 20–50% narrower than
   the envelope at $n\le100$; its misses lean below, and at AUC .95, 50/50 it
   covered .920 at α = .05. One DGP only. It is the standard comparator the
   envelope implicitly claims to beat, and at 500/500 with B = 4,000 the
   envelope does beat it (.980 at width .134 against .963 at .156).
4. **Scale the Beta floor's reach with $n_0$** instead of a fixed 25 order
   statistics. With $J=\max(3,\lfloor n_0/5\rfloor)$: 15/15 covers .874 / .992 /
   1.000 at width .39 / .53 / .67 against the current .63 / .72 / .79. An
   exploratory rule on one DGP, and a parameter change to the method.
5. **Do not tighten the variance-ratio gate.** Requiring a ratio below .5 lost
   2–4 points of coverage at 500/500 for under 2% width. The gate fires on
   Monte Carlo noise in flat regions, and those firings carry real coverage.
   That is also a reproducibility wart: CPU and GPU float differences can flip
   points. A deterministic gate would be cleaner but changes behaviour.
6. **Three α budgets are each spent in full** (bootstrap, rectangle, Beta).
   Folding them is a redesign; double bootstrap is not affordable at
   n = 10,000.

One open discrepancy: the audit's binormal .8, 500/500, B = 4,000 cell covers
.743 / .893 / .980, while the prior run's pooled binormal file at that size has
.863 / .962 / .992. The AUC mix differs and two commits changed evaluation
since, and the audit did not separate the causes. The prior numbers should not
be assumed to transfer.
